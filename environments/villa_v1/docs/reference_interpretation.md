# Summit Estate — reference interpretation

All 30 supplied PNGs were opened and visually inspected individually before modeling. References are preserved; SHA-256 inventory: `reference_inventory.json`. Windows Zone.Identifier streams are metadata, not extra views. No pre-existing model, scripts or assets were present. Work is restricted to villa_v1.

## Architecture and coordinate convention

Metric, Z up; X left/right as seen from arrival, FRONT = −Y, REAR/lake = +Y. The whole image establishes horizontal stepped white-stone volumes, black roof fascias, glazed pavilions, a strongly framed center, warm wood soffits and warm architectural light. It is a visual brief, not a survey or engineering drawing. The design envelope is 108 m wide × 58 m deep including attached garage/service wings, arrival portico and rear terraces. Habitable pavilions are recessed within that envelope so individual rooms retain residential proportions. Heights: B1 −4.2 to 0; 1F 0 to 5.8; 2F 5.8 to 10.0; 3F 10.0 to 14.2 m. Typical slabs 0.30 m, exterior walls 0.32 m, interior walls 0.18–0.24 m. Main paths 3.2 m; service paths 2.6 m.

## Contradictions and decisions

1. Gym close-up shows huge natural-light windows. User explicitly forbids them: use mirrors and wall panels; natural light remains at library courtyard only.
2. 1F overview locates main living centrally and has two guest bedrooms. User requires left living and one modest flexible room: reassign central volume to foyer/piano, retain one flexible room.
3. Tube chandelier views disagree with requested custom circles. Preserve the suspended warm sculptural character with a bespoke multi-ring composition.
4. Some imagery exaggerates atrium height / exterior central tower. Void exists through 1F and 2F only; a continuous 3F slab caps it. Stair and elevator penetrations are confined to enclosed service cores, not an atrium.
5. Master close-ups suggest separate bedroom/closet/bath rooms. Final overview and explicit text prevail: open cabinetry zoning, only WC and arrival/service core enclosed.
6. The master overview places the headboard toward glass while the close-up uses a side wall. Use a low freestanding headboard with an unobstructed bed-to-rear view; no full-height central wall.
7. Overview garage and shoe room lie on opposite sides. Put mudroom at garage connection; extend front service passage to kitchen/pantry/service lift.
8. Wine close-ups imply hundreds of bottles and a large tasting suite. Keep a medium enclosure and four seats, retaining lit rows and wooden racks.
9. Six-car brief exceeds five cars visible in the 1F overview and four in close-up. Model six independent visual vehicles and preserve a clear drive aisle.
10. 108 × 58 m overall footprint is much larger than the sum of stated room areas. Use stepped wings, terraces and landscaped recesses rather than inflating furniture. Dimensions are design assumptions, not measured reference facts.
11. Site scenery is atmospheric and cannot establish geographic location; no georeferencing inferred from decorative image text.

## Lighting design

3000 K living/general light, 2700–2900 K decorative and concealed accents, 3500 K kitchen/laundry task light. Modeled housings, diffusers and emissive strips paired with actual area/spot sources. Layers: cove/ambient, recessed downlights, stone wall washes, shelf/display lighting, task lights, decorative pendants, safe stair/floor-level lines and exterior soffit/planter lighting. Day and dusk environments share fixtures. No RGB lighting.
